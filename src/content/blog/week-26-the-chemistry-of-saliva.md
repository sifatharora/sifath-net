This week in chemistry, we talked about **solutions, solvents, and enzymes** — and I realized saliva is one of the most important chemical systems in the body. I used to think of it as just spit. Now I see it as a carefully balanced solution that protects teeth all day long.

## Saliva Is a Solution

In class, we learned that a solution is a mixture where one substance dissolves evenly in another. Saliva is mostly water (the solvent), with dissolved minerals, proteins, and enzymes. Those dissolved ingredients are what make saliva useful.

Calcium and phosphate ions in saliva help replenish enamel after acid attacks. Mucins keep tissues moist. And enzymes start breaking down food before it even reaches your stomach.

## Enzymes Do the Work

One enzyme that stood out to me is **amylase**, which begins digesting starches in the mouth. Chemistry class explained enzymes as biological catalysts — molecules that speed up reactions without being used up. That means amylase can keep working as long as starch is present.

There's also antibacterial enzymes in saliva that help limit harmful microbes. So saliva isn't passive. It's actively involved in both digestion and defense.

## The Buffer System

Back in Week 24, I wrote about pH and acid attacks. Saliva is the reason your mouth recovers. It contains bicarbonate and other buffers that neutralize acid and push pH back toward neutral.

Without that buffering, every snack would leave enamel under attack for much longer. Hydration matters because low saliva flow means weaker buffering — which is why dry mouth raises cavity risk.

## Final Thoughts

Learning the chemistry of saliva changed how I think about everyday oral health. Drinking water, chewing sugar-free gum, and staying hydrated aren't just "good habits." They're ways of supporting a chemical system that protects your smile constantly.

As someone interested in dentistry, this week reminded me that great dental care starts with understanding the chemistry already happening in the mouth.
