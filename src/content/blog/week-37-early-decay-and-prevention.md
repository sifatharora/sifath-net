One reason I keep returning to prevention is that timing can change what is possible. Tooth decay develops through a process, and its earliest changes are different from an established hole in a tooth.

## Understanding the Difference

According to NIDCR, early decay can sometimes be stopped or reversed as minerals are restored to enamel. Once a cavity forms, the damage is permanent and needs dental treatment.

That distinction helps me understand why the phrase “enamel repair” needs care. It does not mean a missing piece of tooth will simply grow back.

## What This Changes About My Questions

As someone exploring dentistry, I want to get better at explaining differences like this without oversimplifying them. A hopeful explanation still needs to be accurate.

It also makes me curious about how dental professionals identify changes early and decide what to recommend. Prevention involves understanding where someone is in a process, rather than assuming every situation is the same.

This is the kind of science I enjoy writing about: a small distinction that changes the bigger picture. The more carefully I learn the details, the better I can understand why timely care matters.
